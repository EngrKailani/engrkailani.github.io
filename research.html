02 / PROPOSED RESEARCH QUESTIONS

Questions with measurable outcomes.

These are proposed directions informed by my interest in reliable manipulation under degraded vision and changing contact. The specific approaches and candidate metrics have not yet been tested as a doctoral research program.

QUESTION / MANIPULATION

How does modeling error affect robotic manipulation accuracy?

Compare nominal and uncertainty-aware kinematic models under controlled link-length, encoder-offset, and joint-backlash perturbations. Begin with simulation and validate with hardware only if an appropriate test system becomes available.

Candidate measuresTool-position error (mm), orientation error (°), reachability, solver success rate
QUESTION / HUMAN–ROBOT OPERATIONS

When should a robot ask an astronaut for assistance?

Compare supervised and autonomy-first approaches for representative lunar or planetary tool-handling tasks, focusing on useful operator intervention rather than assuming fully independent operation.

Candidate measuresTask completion, operator interventions, response time, collision or constraint violations
QUESTION / SENSING & VERIFICATION

How robust is robot control when sensing becomes incomplete?

Introduce known delays, drift, intermittent samples, and noisy feedback in a repeatable simulation and compare controller responses against a documented nominal baseline.

Candidate measuresTracking RMS error, settling time, recovery rate, constraint violations

Methodological intent: define baseline tasks and operating assumptions first, report uncertainty and failure cases, and distinguish synthetic evidence from instrumented tests.

02B / PROPOSED GRADUATE RESEARCH CONCEPT

Manipulation with incomplete feedback.

A proposed independent simulation study, not a completed experiment, NASA task, or established research novelty.

Research question. Under simulated sensing uncertainty, can estimation-informed control reduce robotic end-effector tracking error or task failures compared with an otherwise comparable nominal controller?

Motivation. My publicly described NASA robotic-arm assessment involved workspace, actuator loading, preliminary controls, and verification planning. That engineering experience motivates my interest in designing manipulation controllers whose performance can be evaluated under uncertainty. The study proposed here is independent future research; it does not describe additional work performed for NASA.

01 / PROPOSED METHOD

Generic simulation

Use a non-project-specific manipulator model with clearly disclosed assumptions. Introduce synthetic noise, delayed measurements, and intermittent feedback. Do not use or imply unreleased NASA geometry, test conditions, or data.

02 / COMPARISON

Reference versus uncertainty-aware approach

Compare a fixed-model control baseline with an estimation-informed alternative over the same predeclared tasks and disturbance cases. Report when either approach fails, not only when it succeeds.

03 / CANDIDATE MEASURES

Quantitative evaluation plan

Evaluate RMS and peak tool-position error, recovery rate following simulated feedback loss, constraint violations, and computation time. These are proposed metrics—not measured results or performance targets.

Research status and NASA boundary: No controller, experiment, numerical result, or new NASA-specific technical detail is claimed by this proposal. For the previously published internship facts and limitations, see the NASA robotic-arm assessment and its affiliation disclaimer.

NASA MARSHALL / OSTEM / INDEPENDENT INTERNSHIP RESEARCH

High-vacuum robotic-arm feasibility

Independently assessed robotic-arm geometry, workspace, joint loading, actuator compatibility and requirements for proposed lunar-dust mitigation testing, with NASA technical mentor oversight.

Documented findings: 34 hierarchical requirements derived from six operational scenarios, and an estimated 3.1 N·m shoulder self-load moment for one horizontal pose. Rocker and actuator masses were excluded, requiring further center-of-gravity analysis before actuator selection.

ROBOTICS / ACADEMIC GROUP ANALYSIS

UR3e robot kinematics

Forward-kinematic and Jacobian analysis for a six-degree-of-freedom industrial manipulator.

Documented finding: The zero-angle configuration produced a rank-five Jacobian, while another analyzed configuration reached full rank. This was mathematical group analysis, not independently validated hardware testing.
