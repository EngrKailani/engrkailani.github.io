PROJECT 01 / 16

At NASA Marshall Space Flight Center, I worked on a legacy robotic arm for lunar-dust mitigation testing. Within that project I used ROS2 to control and calibrate the arm and Thermal Desktop for both steady-state and transient thermal analysis of the arm in high vacuum. The wider assessment connected workspace geometry, actuator constraints, and verification planning.

Engineering details

Challenge

Evaluate whether a robotic arm could meet test-surface access and chamber constraints.

Approach

Use ROS2 for robotic-arm control and calibration, Thermal Desktop for steady-state and transient thermal analysis in high vacuum, and workspace/actuator analysis to organize planned verification across six operational scenarios and 34 system requirements.

Tools

ROS2 (arm control and calibration), Thermal Desktop (steady-state and transient high-vacuum thermal analysis), Creo, robotics, systems engineering, verification planning.

Engineering workstreams and verification scope

Control & calibration

ROS2-based robotic-arm control and calibration formed the motion-control workstream. The portfolio does not publish measured calibration error, repeatability, or final hardware acceptance data.

Thermal modeling

Thermal Desktop was used for steady-state and transient thermal analysis relevant to high-vacuum operation. Numerical model outputs and measured thermal margins are not publicly documented here.

Requirements & constraints

The assessment considered chamber access, workspace geometry, actuator constraints, and 34 system requirements across six operational scenarios. Counts describe project scope, not tests passed.

Verification planning

Verification activities were organized against the requirements and scenarios. Planning and modeling do not establish a completed vacuum qualification campaign or compliance of the physical arm.

Documented outcome

Integrated control/calibration and thermal modeling into the requirements-led evaluation of a high-vacuum robotic-arm application. Those requirements and scenarios describe the assessment scope; vacuum qualification and thermal margins have not been demonstrated.
