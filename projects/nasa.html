NASA Robotic-Arm Assessment

Connecting motion, actuator capability, and thermal constraints for lunar-dust testing.

PROJECT BRIEF

My role and the result

Problem

Evaluate a legacy arm for chamber access, actuator capability, and high-vacuum operation.

My contribution

Worked on ROS2 control and calibration, Thermal Desktop analysis, and requirements-led assessment.

Engineering tools

ROS2, Thermal Desktop, Creo, workspace analysis, and verification planning.

Validation method

Compared calculated shoulder load with estimated actuator capability; organized 34 requirements and six planned scenarios.

Documented result

Identified a potential torque shortfall: 27.23 lbf-in calculated load versus 18.6–24.9 lbf-in estimated capability.

Limitations

Calculated assessment, not measured acceptance testing. Public calibration error, thermal margins, and vacuum qualification are unavailable.

VISUAL ENGINEERING REVIEW

From engineering decisions to visual evidence

These four vector figures reconstruct project concepts from the public portfolio summary. They are illustrative graphics, not NASA-approved engineering drawings, laboratory photographs, solver screenshots, or proof of completed qualification testing.

Engineering decisions

Check mechanical feasibility alongside motion control

A reachable pose also has to be supportable by the actuator. The assessment connected workspace geometry and shoulder-load calculations with the available motor data.

Treat thermal behavior as a separate workstream

Motion control alone cannot establish suitability for high vacuum. Steady-state and transient Thermal Desktop analysis addressed the environmental part of the assessment.

Implementation and verification scope

Control & calibration

Used ROS2 for robotic-arm control and calibration within the assessment. Figure 01 provides a conceptual geometry reference.

Thermal modeling

Used Thermal Desktop for steady-state and transient analysis relevant to high-vacuum operation. Figure 04 illustrates the workstream qualitatively; it is not a solver output.

Requirements & constraints

Organized chamber access, workspace geometry, and actuator constraints against 34 system requirements and six operational scenarios. Figure 03 shows a conceptual public-summary workflow.

Verification planning

Connected planned verification activities to those requirements and scenarios. These counts describe scope, not qualification tests passed. See the conceptual verification map.

Testing and results

The shoulder-load comparison identified a potential actuator-capability shortfall for further engineering review. The 27.23 lbf-in calculated load exceeded the estimated 18.6–24.9 lbf-in capability range. Figure 02 visualizes the comparison. This finding is documented in my résumé and remains an analytical assessment, not a measured torque test.

Next verification step

Validate the actuator assumptions and load cases, measure calibration performance, and complete thermal and vacuum verification against the requirements.

Evidence & limitations

The torque comparison is an analytical finding documented in my résumé. Detailed NASA source figures, calibration error, temperatures, qualified thermal margins, and completed qualification results are not public here. The CAD photo provides design context only.
