PROJECT 01 / 16

At NASA Marshall Space Flight Center, I worked on a legacy robotic arm for lunar-dust mitigation testing. Within that project I used ROS2 to control and calibrate the arm and Thermal Desktop for both steady-state and transient thermal analysis of the arm in high vacuum. The wider assessment connected workspace geometry, actuator constraints, and verification planning.

My contribution / team context

NASA Marshall OSTEM project: used ROS2 for robotic-arm control and calibration and Thermal Desktop for steady-state and transient thermal analysis, alongside systems requirements and workspace assessment.

Engineering challenge

Evaluate whether a robotic arm could meet test-surface access and chamber constraints.

Methods and approach

Use ROS2 for robotic-arm control and calibration, Thermal Desktop for steady-state and transient thermal analysis in high vacuum, and workspace/actuator analysis to organize planned verification across six operational scenarios and 34 system requirements.

Tools and environment

ROS2 (arm control and calibration), Thermal Desktop (steady-state and transient high-vacuum thermal analysis), Creo, robotics, systems engineering, verification planning.

Evidence and limitations

The diagram on the homepage is conceptual. NASA source figures, calibration error, temperatures, qualified thermal margins and completed qualification results are not public here.

Documented outcome

Integrated control/calibration and thermal modeling into the requirements-led evaluation of a high-vacuum robotic-arm application. The 34 requirements and six scenarios describe assessment scope, not completed vacuum qualification or proven thermal margins.
