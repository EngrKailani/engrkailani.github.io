Engineering decisions

Use an external mechanical interface

The clip-on concept applies preload outside the tubing and senses its reaction force. Keeping the sensor outside the fluid pathway was a defining design constraint; translating that force into reliable restriction detection still needed verification.

Coordinate the entire indication path

The integration work connected mechanical contact, analog conditioning, threshold response, and microcontroller supervision. The documented bench outcome was two LED indications; normal, partial-restriction, and occlusion states remained the intended product interface.

Testing and results

The student bench prototype demonstrated a basic path from sensing and threshold response to two indicator LEDs. It established a limited integration proof of concept; it did not establish a clinically reliable three-state occlusion detector.

Next verification step

Characterize preload repeatability, calibrate the force response, and test candidate thresholds across tubing and operating conditions before making any accuracy or clinical-use claim.
