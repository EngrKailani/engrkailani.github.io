ROBOTICS · GROUP ANALYSIS

In a three-person robotics analysis project, we modeled the six-joint UR3e using Denavit–Hartenberg transforms and Jacobian rank analysis. The work identified singular and nonsingular configurations in the team's mathematical model, without independent robot-hardware validation.

Team context

Member of a three-person robot-kinematics analysis team; the project documentation describes shared DH, Jacobian, and singularity analysis.

Engineering details

Challenge

Represent the pose and end-effector velocity of a six-revolute-joint robot and identify singular configurations.

Approach

Develop a six-link DH table, multiply homogeneous transformations, derive the Jacobian, and evaluate its rank with MATLAB. The analyzed zero-angle configuration produced rank 5, indicating a singularity.

Tools

MATLAB, Denavit–Hartenberg convention, forward kinematics, Jacobians, velocity analysis.

KINEMATICS / SINGULARITY REASONING

What the reported Jacobian rank means

Each revolute joint contributes a rigid transformation to the modeled end-effector pose. The geometric Jacobian maps joint rates to translational and angular velocity; reduced rank identifies task-space directions that cannot be produced independently at a given configuration.

Forward kinematicsT₀⁶(q) = A₁(q₁) A₂(q₂) ⋯ A₆(q₆)

Instantaneous differential kinematics[v; ω] = J(q) q̇

Geometric Jacobian, revolute joint iJᵥ,ᵢ = zᵢ₋₁ × (o₆ − oᵢ₋₁); Jω,ᵢ = zᵢ₋₁

Evaluation sequence

  • Assign six consistent Denavit–Hartenberg coordinate frames and link transforms.
  • Form the tool pose by multiplying homogeneous transforms.
  • Assemble the six-column geometric Jacobian from joint axes and origins.
  • Evaluate rank at selected joint vectors in MATLAB and compare singular with nonsingular cases.

What would make this reproducible?

  • The original DH parameter table, exact frame convention and tool offset.
  • Exact joint vectors for every evaluated pose, not simply a pose label.
  • The evaluated Jacobian entries, numerical rank tolerance, and MATLAB implementation.
  • Hardware or independent numerical comparisons if claiming physical-robot accuracy.

Evidence boundary: These are standard mathematical relationships and a visual explanation of the group's reported ranks. The source records do not preserve the complete numeric matrices or original code in this public case study. No new numerical rank calculation or hardware result is claimed.

Interpretation / analytical limitation

Rank is not a complete measure of robot dexterity

The team's reported rank comparison identifies one singular model configuration, but a complete motion-quality assessment requires more than checking whether the Jacobian is full rank.

Numerical tolerance

A computed rank depends on matrix values and the threshold used to classify small singular values. The original matrix and tolerance are not publicly preserved.

Near-singular behavior

Even a full-rank Jacobian may be poorly conditioned, making some end-effector motions demand disproportionately large joint rates. Conditioning was not independently quantified.

Physical constraints

Joint limits, controller behavior, payload and sensor error affect real operation. The reported mathematical ranks do not constitute a hardware safety or accuracy test.

Reproduction requirement: Verify the six-joint model with the actual DH parameters, exact joint vectors, complete Jacobian entries and an explicit rank tolerance. See the reported comparison and equations.

Documented outcome

The group reported Jacobian rank 5 at the analyzed zero-angle configuration and full rank 6 at another evaluated configuration. The distinction identifies a locally singular pose in the mathematical model; it does not establish independent UR3e hardware accuracy, numerical conditioning, or general safety margins.

Evidence & limitations

Project methods and the reported rank comparison follow available group-work records. The original DH table, full numerical Jacobian outputs, exact nonsingular joint vector, numerical rank threshold and independently measured hardware validation are not published here. The technical diagram is a schematic explanation of a reported result, not a recovered experimental plot. Individual mathematical derivations are not separately attributed within the three-person team.

What a reviewer can verify

  • Interpret the reported results: Available: the three-person team's reported rank-five singular and rank-six nonsingular configuration comparison.
  • Recompute the Jacobian: Requires: the team's DH table, frame convention, tool offset, exact joint vectors and numerical tolerance.
  • Validate physical behavior: Not available: independent UR3e hardware trials or a full conditioning and position-error assessment.
